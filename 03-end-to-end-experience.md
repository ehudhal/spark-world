# End-to-end storyboard: from parent discovery to second ride

Nine-screen first-session prototype. Screen names are conceptual, not final layout specifications.

| # | Stage | What the user sees | Core interaction | Product / story purpose |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parent discovery | Short, playful ad/promo of strange ride + character reactions | Tap to learn more | Show delight before technology |
| 2 | Parent landing | Benefit, creative preview, trust/safety message | Try the adventure | Explain who it's for and why it is worth a try |
| 3 | Hand-off | Magical invitation/door to world; no auth for demo | Begin | Transition from parent framing to child's agency |
| 4 | Cinematic | Desaturated, sleepy world; glimpses of old wonders | Watch / skip | Establish longing and promise |
| 5 | Meet Pip | Small, expressive inventor; Spark Machine responds to player | Follow Pip | Form a bond; give player an identity |
| 6 | First mission | Almost comically slow coaster; three contrasting friends | Accept challenge | Emotional reason to build |
| 7 | Build | Large approachable ride-editing options | Select giant hill / twisty turn / funny tunnel | Get first creative win without blank-canvas paralysis |
| 8 | Ride & reward | Characters ride, react; world becomes colorful; Play Spark appears | Collect Spark | Deliver comedy, consequence and gratification |
| 9 | Customize & replay | Accessory stand and character reactions | Equip accessory, play again | Make collectible matter, close repeatable loop |

## Key transitions and UX details
### Parent → kid
The parent-facing page should clarify safety and creative value without foregrounding generative AI. The prototype intentionally has **no authentication**, so the child can start immediately. A production signup/onboarding and parent consent journey must be specified separately.

### Cinematic → agency
Keep exposition minimal: a short, skippable sequence. Ideally the child triggers the Spark Machine rather than merely watching a video about it.

### First mission → creation
Pip's suggestions are optional training wheels. Offer 2–3 large visual choices. For later versions, support both touch editing and constrained voice intents (e.g., “Make it go higher,” “Add a dinosaur tunnel”) as alternative affordances.

### Creation → experience
Convey stakes before the ride with anticipation and staging: the cautious rider checks the harness; the thrill-seeker pumps their fists; the comedian carries a ridiculous prop.

### Experience → reward
Always celebrate the first novel ride. The Spark is a secondary reinforcement to the primary reward: watching recognizable friends have a memorable experience. Rare emotion sparks may appear *in addition* to normal sparks, not as a pass/fail score.

### Reward → customization
First accessory is unlocked instantly, not purchased with a consumable currency. Demonstrate persistence and effect on animation, e.g. a propeller spinning during the next ride.

## Proposed measurement / observation
- Parent understands premise in < 15 seconds (hypothesis to test).
- Child understands why they are building, with little or no reading.
- Time from in-world entry to first meaningful edit.
- % who press Ride after edit; spontaneous laughter; repeat rides.
- % who spontaneously refer to a character by name / show concern.
- % who equip the cosmetic and notice its presence in gameplay.

## Production disclaimers
These nine scenes currently exist as textual specifications and an HTML storyboard. They are not illustrated final screens or a functional game build.
