# Product brief: Spark Worlds

**Status:** Concept and clickable storyboard. **Working title:** Spark Worlds.

## Vision
A whimsical, story-led, child-friendly 3D world-building game. Kids are invited into once-wondrous worlds that have grown dull over generations. They reignite fun and imagination by making experiences for a cast of recurring, expressive creatures. Rather than passive crafting, every creation is experienced by characters, whose joyful and funny reactions make the player's imagination feel consequential.

## Inspirations, not exact references
- *Astro Bot*: playful overarching mission, memorable recurring characters, sense of discovery and delight.
- Pixar/Disney animation and storytelling: clear character desires, emotional arcs, anticipation, readable silhouettes, expressive posing and timing.
- Sandbox creation: safe freedom within meaningful constraints.

Do not copy characters, franchises, visual designs, or storylines from these references.

## Core experience
1. A character presents an emotional reason to create something.
2. The player invents/builds with touch, optionally voice in a future iteration.
3. Characters experience the creation and respond through animation, dialog, sound, humor.
4. The player receives a guaranteed regular **Play Spark** after the first meaningful experience of a new creation, not a subjective AI grade.
5. Rare emotional sparks (e.g., laughter, excitement) occasionally appear as pleasant bonuses.
6. Sparks unlock cosmetic accessories for the existing cast. Player sees accessories in subsequent experiences.
7. Kid iterates and repeats; over time the park and larger world regain color, life and fun.

## Audience and roles
- **Parent** discovers and evaluates the product, understands creative value and safety, and makes the hand-off.
- **Child** plays as the new World Maker. Exact target age and hardware platform are not yet decided. iPad/touch-first was suggested as a hypothesis, not a locked requirement.

## First world / vertical slice
The amusement park, with a formerly sleepy roller coaster. The player can modify the track through a few approachable choices (e.g., big hill, twisty turn, funny tunnel), put characters on the ride, watch their reactions, earn their first Play Spark, and unlock an accessory.

## Future worlds (not MVP)
- Haunted house: traps, surprises, spooky-but-playful character reactions.
- Fart museum: absurd exhibits and interactions.
- Expanded amusement park: larger coaster systems, different POVs, ride characters with contrasting reactions.
- Additional characters and broader personality/visual customization; not part of initial prototype.

## Scope boundaries
**First clickable storyboard:** parent acquisition/landing, optional parent context, hand-off, story introduction, character introductions, first mission, simplified build choice, ride feedback, reward, accessory, replay.

**Not in first clickable storyboard:** accounts/authentication, purchases, real 3D rendering, voice input, AI-generated assets, multiplayer, full world editor, physically simulated coaster.

**Later playable vertical slice:** one functioning 3D amusement-park scene, basic construction affordances, character animation/reactions and reward loop.

## Guiding decisions
- Warm, humorous, emotionally legible storytelling rather than grim rescue stakes.
- World is not destroyed; fun and experimentation have faded from culture.
- Curated recurring cast with fixed personalities, lightweight appearance customization.
- Rewards recognize participation and a new meaningful experience, rather than rating the child's creative output.
- Rare emotional sparks are surprise variants, not a prerequisite for progress.
- Cosmetics unlock permanently instead of a potentially frustrating consumable-spend system.
- Expand worlds only after the first five-minute loop produces delight in playtesting.

## Open questions
- Specific age band, iPad/browser/console priority, reading level, accessibility.
- Final title and art direction, including distinct look rather than a direct imitation of Pixar.
- Exact character roster, names, appearance and canonical dialogue beyond Pip.
- Reward cadence beyond first ride; what constitutes a meaningfully new creation.
- How voice/touch requests translate to bounded construction commands.
- Parent controls, privacy/data practices, monetization, audio/language accessibility.
